From Rayven · For Planner Friends

I want your stories in the next edition.

While I was writing the second edition of my event management textbook, I kept reaching for the same thing: real stories from working planners. Mine are in there (the reception minimum we met by raffling off wine, the ballroom poles that swallowed half a room’s sightlines). However, one planner’s stories only cover so much ground. Yours cover the rest, and students remember the war stories long after they forget the definitions.

So here is the whole ask. Skim the chapter list below; it takes about two minutes. Under each chapter are a few prompts, the kind of thing planners trade over drinks. If one of them surfaces a memory (a save, a disaster, a lesson that cost you money), that is a story I want. Tell it however is easiest: a voice memo, a rambling text, a phone call. Polish is my job, not yours, and it runs with your name on it. [Yes, your name, in an actual textbook.]

Timing, honestly: the second edition is already deep in drafting, with plans to publish late Fall 2026. I know schedules are crazy, so any stories that don’t make the editorial deadline will be added to the third edition. So not to worry! This is community over competition, a textbook by planners for planners.

The Chapters

Thirteen chapters, each with its territory and a few questions to jog the memory.

01 · History of Events & Event Studies

How events are defined and categorized: festivals, parades, heritage commemorations, mega events, life-cycle celebrations, corporate gatherings, and why one event is always doing three jobs at once.

What was the event that made you fall in love with this industry?

Have you worked a festival or hallmark event that reshaped its whole city for a week?

Ever planned something where the host’s purpose and the guests’ reasons for coming were two different sets of expectations?

02 · Business & Trade Events

Meetings, conventions, expos, and incentive trips; corporations, associations, and government entities as hosts, each with their own economics and their own rules.

What is the strangest rule you have worked under at a corporate, association, or government event? Per diem limits, travel reimbursements, private internet networks?

Ever had an exhibit floor fund an event’s entire budget?

Is there anything specific about working with this sector of events that distinguishes them from other events like weddings or festivals?

03 · Careers in the Event Industry

Who hires planners (venues, suppliers, corporations, associations, government), job titles and role chains, niche markets, compensation, and certifications.

How did you actually land your first planning job? The real version.

Did you fall into a niche (pharma, legal, sports, nonprofit) that nobody told you existed?

What did a certification, or skipping one, change about your career?

04 · Transforming Events into Experiences

The theory chapter: flow, the experience economy, authenticity, liminality, communitas. The machinery underneath what guests actually feel.

What do you do to create “experiences” that go beyond everyday events?

What are some ways you facilitate connection among attendees at your events?

What is an activity you designed that people still bring up years later?

05 · The Planning Process

Pricing your services (flat, percentage, hourly), the five phases from research through evaluation, timelines that run to hundreds of tasks, and what post-event data is actually for.

Which pricing model did you start with, and what made you change it?

What is the first step you take when a new client comes to you to plan an event from scratch?

What does your timeline system look like now, and which disaster/slip up helped you design it?

06 · Event Design

Theme, setting, atmosphere, and the total sensory experience; design budgets, satisfiers and dissatisfiers, tents and generators, and the pitfalls between vision and load-in.

What is a design detail guests genuinely noticed, and the expensive one they never did?

Ever built a venue from nothing: a field, a backyard, a tent, a generator?

What is a theme or design element a client wanted that you had to lovingly talk them out of?

07 · Communication, Leadership & Human Resources

Communication styles, relationship selling, client relations and difficult clients, vendor and volunteer management, and protecting your own health and reputation while you do it.

What is the difficult-client story you still tell? Names removed, of course.

Which vendor saved you once, and what do you do for them now?

What is the red flag you now catch in the very first consultation with clients or vendors?

08 · Event Finance & Budgeting

Financial goals, revenue and registration mixes, cash flow, F&B minimums and service charges, break-even math, and the wrinkles international events add.

What budget line has blindsided you: service charges, overtime, exchange rates, unexpected hotel fees?

Ever had the registration mix wreck a budget projection that looked solid on paper?

How did you learn to keep cash flowing before the deposits arrive?

09 · Food & Beverage Services

Caterer types, BEOs, guarantees, bar programs, dietary restrictions, attrition, and the logistics of feeding a few hundred people on a schedule.

Tell me about a guarantee call you got exactly right, or painfully wrong.

Is there a line you now put on every BEO because of one specific event?

What is your best bar story: drink tickets, consumption math, a best man who hijacked a golfcart and then went back to the bar?

10 · Venue Layout & AV

Function rooms, capacity charts and sightlines, mics and mixers, screens and projection, lighting, and the added machinery of hybrid events.

What is the site visit that saved you, or the one you skipped and paid for? Or just a funny one. My fav is visiting a Marriott for a site visit during a spicy comic-con.

Got a mic story? Feedback, dead batteries, a hot mic at the worst possible moment?

Have you run a hybrid event where the remote audience got the better (or worse) show?

11 · Event Contracts

RFPs, negotiation, attrition and force majeure, smart clauses, and the verbiage that decides who pays when plans change.

Which clause has saved you real money, or cost you real money by its absence?

What is the best concession you ever negotiated into a contract?

Do you have a force majeure story from 2020 (or another year) that you can finally laugh about?

12 · Marketing Events

Target audiences and segmentation, StoryBrand messaging, print and digital channels, email lists, and finding out what actually drove registration.

What is the campaign that actually filled the seats, and how do you know?

What is your favorite piece of event marketing and why?

How did you find out where your attendees really hear about you?

13 · Risk Management

Safety and security, negligence, the risk management cycle, event safety and security plans, insurance and liquor liability, and preparing for the day you hope never comes.

Have you made a weather call with real money on the line?

What close call permanently changed your checklist?

What did you learn about insurance or liability the expensive way?
