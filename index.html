From the Founder

Rayven Crisafulli, PhD, CMP
Founder, Resonance Co.

Events have been my work for thirteen years: eight as a planner, five teaching event management to university students, and eventually as a doctoral researcher chasing one question: why some events feel like the best day of your life, the kind you don’t want to end, while others are pleasant enough, but lacking that je ne sais quoi, that something special that made it feel a bit magical.

It isn’t budget, and it isn’t aesthetics. I’ve watched a modest wedding move a room to tears and a lavish, beautifully styled one leave everyone cold, and I’ve seen the same split at conferences. The difference lives beneath all of that: whether an event makes you feel something, connect to the people in the room, and attach meaning to the whole of it.

That question became my dissertation, and then it became Resonance Co. This space was built for planners who see an event as more than a series of logistics, but as an opportunity to tell a story through the medium of an experience. If that is the kind of planner you are, or the kind you’re becoming, you belong here.

On Resonance

Resonance is relational.

Resonance, in sociology, is a call and response: a genuine engagement between a person and the world around them. My doctoral research carried that idea into how events are designed.

In our field, resonance emerges from intentional design that shows a deep knowledge of the people in the room, rather than from generic spectacle. The design surfaces the deeper symbolism and meaning already held in the elements of the experience. We build the conditions for it through moments of surprise and delight: a design element that nods to something only these guests would catch. Then we hold those threads while our attendees live out their story inside the experience.

Resonance Mapping is how we design for that on purpose rather than by instinct alone, along three axes: identity, who is in the room and how they see themselves; experience, how the environment and activities create mental and physical engagement; and story, the arc that moves them through it. Tend all three, and you create the conditions for the kind of event people carry with them, the one they don’t want to leave.
